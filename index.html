01 / OVERVIEW

Everyday appliances.
Everyday independence.

A flat touch panel. An unreadable display. A sequence of steps to remember.

Modern appliances can turn everyday tasks into accessibility barriers. Poros connects appliance knowledge with real-time perception, helping blind and low vision (BLV) users understand what to do, find the right control, and confirm what happened.

Abstract

Modern home appliances pose significant accessibility barriers for individuals with visual impairments, driven by increasing functional complexity and minimalist design trends. Drawing on formative interviews with blind and low vision (BLV) participants, we propose Poros, a novel system providing perception-enabled interactive assistance to facilitate seamless appliance interaction.

Our approach features an automated instruction generator that models appliance operation logic directly from user manuals to synthesize executable guidance for arbitrary tasks. Integrated into an Augmented Reality (AR) wearable, Poros provides real-time auditory feedback synchronized with the user’s hand gestures and the appliance’s display state. A user study with quantitative analysis and qualitative insights shows that our approach enhances user independence, enabling the successful execution of complex, multi-step operations that were previously considered inaccessible.

A CLOSER LOOK From appliance manuals to executable instructions+

The instruction generator extracts buttons, task chains, and parameter-update rules from appliance manuals. These form an operation state machine, refined through validation on the physical appliance. For a given task, the system computes a sequence of button presses to reach the target settings.

89.8% task-chain extraction accuracy across 59 operation procedures from nine appliance manuals.

We compared Poros with tactile markers and the conventional appliance interface. Fourteen BLV participants were recruited; valid trials from 12 participants for simple tasks and 10 for hard tasks were analyzed after incomplete sessions due to technical issues were excluded.

Poros supported successful task completion and higher confidence, especially on hard tasks. Completion times were numerically lower than with markers, but the differences were not statistically significant.
